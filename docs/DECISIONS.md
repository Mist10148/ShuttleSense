| D43 | 2026-11-20 | *Builds on D38.* Shuttle positions are located to a fraction of a pixel and the track is fitted flight by flight. The heatmap peak is placed by a 3-point Gaussian fit (the weighted centroid on a flat top), and when several blobs pass the threshold the one with the largest peak x area wins. Outlier and burst distances scale with the image row (0.7x at the top, 1.2x at the bottom). A new step (`tracking/trajectory.py`) splits the track at sharp turns, drops points that one quadratic through both sides' neighbours misses, smooths each flight with a Huber-weighted local quadratic (never across a turn, a landing or a long gap; turn points are kept as detected) and fills gaps up to 20 frames only when one quadratic flight fits both ends. Hits are found on that smoothed track and timed where the flights before and after meet, still an integer frame in the record. The court corners, once snapped, get a least-squares homography over every painted line point found, with its reprojection error logged. All of it is on by default and can be switched off in `stages.tracking.precision`, `stages.hits` and `stages.court` The flight fitting runs on uploaded clips (the tracking stage); live and Watch windows call `rectify` every tick and skip it unless `stages.tracking.precision.trajectory_live` is set, so their per-tick cost is unchanged. | A Kalman or particle filter on the track; retrain TrackNetV3 with sub-pixel targets; scipy splines; keep the 4-corner homography | D38 turned down a Kalman filter because its noise settings would need labelled positions. These steps need none: each removes a failure that physics rules out (a point off a smooth flight, a jump too far for the depth, a corner rounded by smoothing), and each is checked on synthetic flights with ground truth: track error fell from about 40 px to 7 px on corrupted rallies, the peak error on Gaussian heatmaps from about 0.1 px to under 0.01 px, noise on a clean flight from 2.8 px to 1.5 px, the court corners from 0.5 px to 0.2 px. NumPy and OpenCV only. Accuracy on real footage is still unmeasured ([EVALUATION](EVALUATION.md)); `scripts/eval_track_smoothness.py` reports jitter and hit counts from a record.json without labels | M1 |
| D44 | 2026-11-20 | The court heatmap counts time, not frames: each frame in a rally adds 1 / fps seconds at a player's position, spread by a Gaussian in metres (0.30 m at the near baseline, widening to 0.45 m at the far one) on a 0.25 m grid. Positions pinned to the 2 m clamp edge are left out. Doubles partners are merged per side. The backend writes the same grids, and a landing density from the rallies' landings, to `heatmap.json` next to summary.json; the app draws its own grid from the record by the same method. Player positions are smoothed with a median within runs of frames only, never across a gap, and steps are capped at 8 m/s; the floor point uses the lower ankle when the other foot is clearly lifted | Keep 0.5 m cells with a fixed 3x3 kernel; serve the backend grid to the app; a moving average over gaps | Frame counts made the heatmap depend on the frame rate, and a 3x3 cell kernel spread a far-court player as little as a near one although the far foot point is several times less certain. A clamped position is a known wrong place, so counting it piled heat on the edge. Averaging across a gap blended where a player was before and after it. The app keeps its own grid so an older backend and live sessions still have a heatmap; `heatmap.json` is for exports and is not served by the API yet | M1 |
| D45 | 2026-11-25 | Automatic points are awarded only from a landing the referee can stand behind. UNCERTAIN, so no point, when the rest is more than `max_out_m` (2 m) outside the lines or an IN landing is within the band of the net.

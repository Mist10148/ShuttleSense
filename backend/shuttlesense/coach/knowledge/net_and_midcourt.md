# Net shots, lifts, pushes and drives

## Net shot
A net shot is a soft stroke from your front court that tumbles just over the net into the opponent's front court. Meet the shuttle as high and as early as possible, ideally near the net tape: the higher the contact, the more options you have and the tighter the shot. Hold the racket in front with a relaxed grip and use the fingers and a gentle push rather than a swing. Spinning (tumbling) net shots are hard to return cleanly. Reach with the racket arm toward the net before the body arrives; ShuttleSense checks this as `racket_arm_forward`.

## Lunge
Most front-court strokes are played from a lunge onto the racket-side leg. The front knee bends and stays above or slightly behind the toes, the heel lands first, and the back leg trails to keep balance. A deep, stable lunge lets you reach the shuttle early and push back to base quickly. A shallow lunge with a straight knee usually means a late or stretched contact. ShuttleSense checks `lunge_knee_bend` on net shots and lifts.

## Lift
A lift sends a shuttle from your front court high and deep to the opponent's back court. It is a defensive reset when you cannot play a tight net shot. Get under the shuttle with contact below shoulder height, swing up with a forearm rotation, and aim high enough that the opponent cannot intercept it and deep enough to reach the back line. A short lift is the most common source of easy smashes for the opponent. ShuttleSense checks `wrist_below_shoulder` on lifts.

## Push
A push is a flat, controlled shot from the front or mid court that sends the shuttle to the opponent's mid court, often down the side lines or between doubles partners. Use a short backswing and a firm, compact action. Pushes work well against an opponent who has rushed forward or a doubles pair that is not set.

## Drive
A drive is a fast, flat shot that crosses the net at about head height and travels roughly parallel to the floor, mostly in doubles mid-court exchanges. Keep the racket up in front of the body, use short swings driven by the forearm and fingers rather than a big arm swing, and reset the racket quickly between shots. Aim at the opponent's racket-side shoulder or the space behind a front player.

## Defending a smash
Stand with feet wider than shoulder width, knees bent and racket in front at about waist height, often on the backhand side, since most body smashes are easier to cover there. Block short to the net to make the attacker move forward, or drive flat if the smash was weak. Lift long and high when you need time to recover.

# Rules of badminton (BWF Laws, summary)

## Scoring system
A match is the best of three games. Every rally scores a point for the side that wins it (rally-point scoring). A game is won by the first side to 21 points. At 20-all, the side that first gains a two-point lead wins the game. At 29-all, the side that scores the 30th point wins the game. The side winning a game serves first in the next game.

## Intervals and change of ends
When the leading score reaches 11 points, the players may take an interval of up to 60 seconds. Between games there is an interval of up to 120 seconds. Players change ends at the end of the first game, at the end of the second game if there is a third, and in the third game when the leading score reaches 11 points.

## Singles service courts
In singles, the server serves from the right service court when the server's score is even (0, 2, 4...) and from the left when it is odd. The singles service court is long and narrow: it extends to the back boundary line and uses the inner side lines. If the server wins the rally, the server scores and serves again from the other service court; if the receiver wins, the receiver scores and becomes the server.

## Doubles service courts
In doubles, the serving side serves from the right service court when its score is even and from the left when it is odd. The doubles service court is short and wide: it ends at the doubles long service line and uses the outer side lines. Only the serving side's players change service courts, and only when they win a point while serving. When the receiving side wins a rally, it scores and gains the serve; there is only one serve per side in rally-point scoring.

## Service rules and service faults
The server and receiver stand within diagonally opposite service courts without touching the boundary lines, and part of both feet of each must stay in contact with the court in a stationary position until the serve is delivered. The server's racket must first hit the base of the shuttle, and the whole shuttle must be below 1.15 metres from the court surface at the moment it is hit. Once the server starts the forward movement of the racket, it must continue until the serve is delivered; undue delay is a fault. A serve that lands outside the receiver's service court, or does not cross the net, is a fault. A server who misses the shuttle while attempting to serve commits a fault.

## Faults during play
It is a fault if the shuttle lands outside the boundaries of the court, passes through or under the net, fails to pass over the net, touches the ceiling or side walls, or touches a player's body or clothing. It is also a fault if a player hits the shuttle twice in succession, if partners both hit it in succession, if a player touches the net or its supports with the racket or body while the shuttle is in play, if the racket or body crosses over the net into the opponent's court (except a follow-through after contact that started on the hitter's own side), or if a player obstructs or distracts the opponent. A shuttle landing on a line is in.

## Lets
A let stops play and the rally is replayed without a change of score. Lets include an unforeseen or accidental occurrence, the server serving before the receiver is ready, the shuttle getting caught in and remaining suspended on top of the net after passing over it (except during service), the shuttle disintegrating with the base completely separating from the rest, and a line judge being unsighted when the umpire cannot make a decision.

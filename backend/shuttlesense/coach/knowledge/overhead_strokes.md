# Overhead strokes: clear, smash and drop

## Shared overhead preparation
All three overhead strokes start from the same preparation, which is what makes them hard to read. Turn side-on early so the non-racket shoulder points toward the shuttle, raise the non-racket arm to track the shuttle and balance the body, and bring the racket up and back with the elbow at about shoulder height. Load weight onto the back (racket-side) leg. Keep the grip relaxed until just before contact; squeezing the grip at the last moment adds speed without tension.

Common faults: letting the shuttle drop too low before hitting, staying square to the net, and starting the swing with the arm instead of the legs and trunk.

## Contact point for overheads
Hit at the highest comfortable point, slightly in front of the racket shoulder, with the hitting arm close to straight. A high contact gives a steeper angle on smashes and drops and more length on clears. A bent elbow at contact usually means the player was late getting behind the shuttle, so the fix is often footwork, not the arm. In ShuttleSense the rules `contact_above_head` and `elbow_extension` check these two cues from 2D pose.

## Clear
The clear sends the shuttle high and deep to the opponent's back line. The defensive (high) clear buys time to recover to base; the attacking clear is flatter and faster, aimed just over the opponent's reach to push them back. Swing up and through, rotate the trunk, and pronate the forearm through contact. Aim for the shuttle to drop vertically near the back boundary. A short clear in singles invites a smash, so length matters more than speed.

## Smash
The smash is the main attacking stroke: steep, fast and downward. Generate power from the legs, hips and trunk rotation, then a fast forearm pronation and a late squeeze of the grip. Contact slightly further in front of the body than for a clear to angle the shuttle down. Aim at the body (racket-side hip or shoulder), the side lines, or the gap between doubles partners. A jump smash adds angle but costs recovery time; in singles a well-placed half smash is often more useful than maximum speed. Speeds in ShuttleSense are 2D estimates, so compare them between your own sessions rather than with published records.

## Drop shot
The drop shot is a soft overhead that falls close to the net in the opponent's front court. Disguise it with the same preparation as a clear or smash, then slow the racket head just before contact. A slice drop (cutting across the base) falls faster and steeper; a block drop is softer. Take the shuttle early and above the shoulder so it travels downward, and keep the follow-through short to control the pace. ShuttleSense checks `contact_above_shoulder` and `short_follow_through` on drops.

## Recovery after an overhead
After the stroke, the racket-side leg comes through (a scissor kick when jumping) and the player pushes back toward base at once. A good overhead is wasted if the player stays at the back watching it. Recover to a position that covers the likely replies: after a smash, move slightly forward, because the reply often comes short or flat.

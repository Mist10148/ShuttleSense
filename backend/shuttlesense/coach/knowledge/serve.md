# Serve and return of serve

## Low serve
The low serve is the standard serve in doubles and common in singles. Aim to pass just over the tape and land near the front service line. Use a short, controlled push with a relaxed grip, usually on the backhand in doubles, and keep the same preparation every time. A consistent contact point matters more than power.

## High serve
The high serve in singles travels high and lands near the back boundary line, falling almost vertically so it is hard to attack. Contact with an underarm swing and follow through upward. A short high serve gives the receiver an easy smash.

## Flick serve
The flick serve looks like a low serve but is pushed quickly over the receiver's head to the back of the service court. It punishes a receiver who rushes the net. Use it sparingly so it stays a surprise.

## Legal service basics
At the moment of service the whole shuttle must be below 1.15 metres from the court surface when hit (BWF Law 9.1.6), and the racket must first hit the base of the shuttle. Server and receiver stand inside diagonally opposite service courts without touching the lines, and part of both feet must stay in contact with the court in a stationary position until the serve is delivered. Once the server starts the forward swing, it must continue in one movement. ShuttleSense's `contact_below_waist` and `stable_feet` cues are camera-space coaching hints, not fault calls: one 2D camera cannot measure 1.15 metres or foot contact.

## Serve routine
Use the same short routine before every serve: check the score and your service court, set your feet, breathe, look at the target, serve. A fixed routine reduces faults under pressure and stops you rushing after a lost rally.

## Return of serve
Stand close to the front service line in doubles and a little further back in singles, racket up, weight on the front foot but ready to push back. Attack low serves early with a tight net shot or a push, and attack loose serves with a downward shot. Against high serves in singles, move back early and hit from behind the shuttle.

# Footwork, base position and split step

## Base position
The base is the place you return to between shots, from which every corner is reachable in a similar number of steps. In singles it is roughly the middle of your half, slightly behind the centre; it shifts a little toward where the opponent is most likely to hit. In doubles the base depends on the formation (front and back when attacking, side by side when defending). Recover toward base after each shot, but do not stop moving when you get there.

## Split step
The split step is a small hop that lands with the feet slightly wider than the shoulders just as the opponent hits. Landing at that moment loads the legs, so the first step in any direction is explosive. Too early and you are flat-footed when they hit; too late and you are still in the air. Watch the opponent's racket, not only the shuttle, and time the landing to their contact. ShuttleSense does not measure split-step timing yet: it needs a footwork signal the pipeline does not compute.

## Moving to the front
Use chasse or running steps to cover the distance, then finish with a lunge onto the racket-side leg. Keep the chest up and the racket in front. Push off the front heel to recover backward.

## Moving to the back
Turn side-on immediately and use chasse steps or a crossover step to get behind the shuttle. For the forehand rear corner, finish with the racket-side leg back so you can hit and transfer weight forward. For the backhand rear corner, take it round-the-head if you can get there in time, otherwise turn and play a backhand clear or drop.

## Moving to the sides
For mid-court side shots, a single wide step or a jump to the side is often enough. Keep the racket up in front so you can block or drive without a backswing.

## Common footwork faults
Standing flat-footed between shots; reaching with the arm instead of moving the feet, which causes many bent-elbow overheads and shallow lunges; recovering too slowly after a lunge; and crossing the feet in a way that leaves you off balance at contact.

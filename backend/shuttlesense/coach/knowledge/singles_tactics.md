# Singles tactics

## Use the four corners
Singles is a game of moving the opponent. Combine length (clears and lifts to the back) with short shots (drops and net shots) so the opponent has to cover the longest distances. A rally that alternates rear and front corners tires the opponent faster than one played side to side.

## Length first
Deep clears and lifts keep the opponent away from the net and give you time to recover. If your clears or lifts land in the mid court, the opponent can attack from a strong position. When under pressure, a high, deep shot is usually the safest choice.

## When to attack
Smash when you are balanced, behind the shuttle and the opponent is out of position or the reply is short. Smashing from deep in the court or off balance often costs more than it gains, because the opponent can block to the net while you are still recovering. Follow a smash forward to cut off a weak block.

## Patterns that work
Clear to the backhand rear corner, then attack the reply. Drop to one front corner, then push or lift to the opposite rear corner. Play a tight net shot and be ready to kill a loose net reply. Vary the pace: a slow drop after several fast clears is hard to read.

## Reducing unforced errors
Most club rallies are lost by errors rather than winners. Aim a little inside the lines (a margin of about half a metre) when you are not under pressure, clear high enough to pass the opponent's reach, and play net shots higher over the tape when you are stretched. Count your errors per game: if more rallies end with your shot out or in the net than with the opponent's, reduce risk before adding speed.

## Reading the score
Play safer when leading late in a game and take measured risks when far behind. After the interval at 11, reset with a simple plan: one thing to keep doing and one thing to change.

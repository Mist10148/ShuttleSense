# Doubles tactics and rotation

## Attack formation
When your side is hitting down, play front and back. The rear player smashes and drops; the front player stands around the front service line, racket up, and intercepts weak blocks and drives to finish the rally. The front player should not cover the rear court, and should stay out of the line of the partner's smash.

## Defence formation
When your side has to lift, move to side by side, each covering a half of the court from front to back. Stand a little behind the middle of the court, racket in front, ready to block or drive. Both players defend the smash aimed at their own half; the shuttle down the middle is usually taken by the player with the forehand, or as agreed before the match.

## Rotation
Change from defence to attack whenever your side gets a chance to hit down: the player who hits the shuttle down follows the shot forward or stays back, and the partner moves to the opposite position so you end in front and back. Change from attack to defence when you have to lift: the player who lifts moves to the side on the same side as the lift (straight lift, same side; cross lift, the opposite side), and the partner takes the other half. Talk to each other: short calls such as "mine", "yours" and "back" prevent clashes and gaps.

## Serving and receiving in doubles
Most serves are low, so the receiver can attack early. After a low serve, the server stays at the net and the partner covers the back. After a flick or a high serve, move straight into side-by-side defence. Receivers should look to attack the serve downward or play a tight net reply to seize the attack.

## Targets in doubles
Hit down the middle between partners to cause hesitation, smash at the body or the racket-side hip, and keep drives flat so the opponents cannot attack them. Avoid lifting to the middle of the court where both opponents can smash.

## Mixed doubles
The usual pattern puts the stronger net player at the front and the stronger smasher at the back, but rotation still applies when the rally demands it. Each pair should agree its own plan before the match.

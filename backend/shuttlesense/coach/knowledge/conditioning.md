# Warm-up, conditioning and recovery

## Warm-up before playing
Spend 10 to 15 minutes warming up before hard hitting: light jogging or skipping to raise the heart rate, dynamic mobility for the ankles, hips, trunk and shoulders (leg swings, lunges with a twist, arm circles), then a few shadow footwork movements and gentle rallying before smashing. Static stretching is better kept for after the session.

## Badminton-specific fitness
Badminton is repeated short bursts with short rests, so train intervals: for example 20 to 30 seconds of shadow footwork or court sprints followed by a similar rest, repeated several times. Add leg strength (squats, split squats, step-ups), calf and ankle work, core stability, and shoulder strength for overhead strokes. Quality of movement matters more than volume.

## Cool-down and recovery
After playing, walk or jog lightly for a few minutes, then stretch the calves, quadriceps, hamstrings, hips and shoulders gently. Drink water through and after the session. Sleep and rest days are part of training; play hard sessions on alternate days when possible.

## Training load
Increase the amount and intensity of training gradually. A sudden jump in hours on court, many jump smashes in a row, or new shoes on a different surface are common times for overuse problems. Keep a simple log of session length and how your body felt.
